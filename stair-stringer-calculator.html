How to Calculate & Lay Out Stair Stringers (2026 IRC Building Code Guide)

Cutting stair stringers from 2×12 dimensional lumber is one of the highest-stakes tasks in residential carpentry and deck building. If a single riser varies by more than 3/8 inch (9.5 mm) from the rest of the flight, the staircase will fail municipal framing inspection under IRC Section R311.7.5.1 and create a severe trip hazard. This interactive Stair Stringer Calculator eliminates layout errors by computing exact fractional framing-square settings, bottom-riser tread drop, remaining 2×12 throat depth, and cumulative diagonal hook marks to the nearest 1/16 inch (or millimeter).

1. The 4 Golden Rules of Stair Stringer Math

Before hooking a framing square onto a 2×12 board, every carpenter and DIY builder must verify four geometric constraints:

  • Rule 1 — Always Round to a Whole Number of Risers: Divide your total vertical floor-to-floor rise by your ideal target riser height (typically 7.25" for indoor stairs or 7.0"–7.5" for exterior deck steps). Round to the nearest whole integer N, then divide the total rise by N to get the exact equal riser height.
  • Rule 2 — Drop the Bottom Riser by One Tread Thickness: Because finished treads sit on top of every horizontal notch, the very first step off the lower floor gets raised by the thickness of its tread (1 1/2" for 2× lumber or 1" for 5/4 decking), whereas the top landing floor is already at finished height. Cutting the bottom plumb cut shorter by exactly one tread thickness makes all finished steps identical.
  • Rule 3 — Verify Effective Stringer Throat Depth (≥ 3 1/2"): A nominal 2×12 board measures 11 1/4" (286 mm) wide. When you notch out a 7 1/4" rise and 10 1/2" run, the perpendicular notch depth removes approximately 6.04" of wood, leaving 5.21" of solid structural throat. Never allow throat depth to fall below 3.5" on unsupported spans.
  • Rule 4 — Check Blondel's Ergonomic Formula (2R + T = 24" to 25"): Even if a stair passes minimum code, a combination like a 7 3/4" rise with a 10" tread (2R + T = 25.5") feels steep and tiring. Aim for 24.5" to 25.0" for effortless foot rhythm.
Number of Risers (N) = round( Total_Rise / Target_Riser )
Exact Riser Height (R) = Total_Rise / N
Bottom Cut Riser = R - Tread_Thickness
Step Hypotenuse (D) = √( R² + Tread_Run² )
2×12 Remaining Throat = 11.25" - ( (R × Tread_Run) / D )

2. Worked Jobsite Example: 108-Inch Basement Staircase

Suppose you are framing a basement staircase with a total finished floor-to-floor height of 108 inches (9 feet) using 2×12 stringers and 1 1/2" thick treads on a flush top mount:

  1. Step Count: Dividing 108" ÷ 7.25" = 14.89. Rounding to 15 risers gives an exact riser height of 108" ÷ 15 = 7.20" (7 3/16" on your framing square tongue).
  2. Tread Count & Total Run: For a flush mount, 15 risers require 14 treads. At 10 1/2" per tread, the total horizontal floor space required is 14 × 10.5" = 147" (12' 3").
  3. Bottom Riser Drop: Subtracting the 1 1/2" tread thickness from the first riser gives 7.20" - 1.50" = 5.70" (5 11/16") for the bottom plumb cut.
  4. Lumber Stock Length: Each step spans √(7.20² + 10.50²) = 12.73" along the board edge. Across 14 treads plus the top plumb tail, you need 184.4" (15' 4 3/8"), meaning you should purchase 16-foot 2×12 #1 Southern Yellow Pine or Douglas Fir stock.

4. Frequently Asked Questions

What is the 2026 IRC building code for stair riser height and tread depth?

Under International Residential Code (IRC R311.7.5), the maximum riser height for residential stairs is 7 3/4 inches (196 mm), and the minimum tread depth (run) is 10 inches (254 mm) measured horizontally from nosing to nosing. Additionally, the greatest riser height within any flight of stairs cannot exceed the smallest by more than 3/8 inch (9.5 mm).

Why do you subtract tread thickness from the bottom riser of a stair stringer?

You must drop the bottom of the stair stringer (called 'dropping the stringer') by the exact thickness of one finished tread (typically 1 inch for 5/4 decking or 1 1/2 inches for 2x12 lumber). Once the finished treads are installed on top of all steps, the first step rises by one tread thickness and every subsequent step shifts up equally, making all finished risers identical.

What is minimum throat depth on a cut 2x12 stair stringer?

The effective throat depth is the narrowest strip of uncut wood remaining along the bottom edge of a notched 2x12 stringer (actual width 11 1/4 inches). Most building codes and structural guidelines require at least 3 1/2 inches (89 mm) of solid uncut throat remaining beneath the notches to prevent splitting under load.

What is Blondel's comfort formula (2R + T) for stairs?

French architect François Blondel established that a natural human stride on stairs satisfies 2 × Riser Height + 1 × Tread Depth = 24 to 25 inches (610 to 635 mm). For example, a 7 1/4 inch riser paired with a 10 1/2 inch tread yields (2 × 7.25) + 10.5 = 25.0 inches, producing a safe, effortless climb.

How many stair stringers do I need for a 36-inch or 48-inch wide staircase?

For standard 2x lumber treads, stringers should be spaced no more than 16 to 18 inches on center, meaning a 36-inch residential stair uses 3 stringers (left, center, right) and a 48-inch deck stair uses 4 stringers. Composite decking treads typically require stringers every 12 inches on center.
