Plywood Sheet Cut-List Optimizer (Kerf-Aware 4×8 Panel Layout Calculator)

Breaking down 4×8 sheets of cabinet-grade Baltic Birch, maple plywood, or 3/4" MDF without a visual cutting diagram wastes both expensive sheet stock and shop hours. This browser-based 2D Plywood Cut-List Optimizer uses a strip-first guillotine packing algorithm that respects real table-saw and track-saw rip cuts while automatically deducting your exact saw blade kerf (1/8", 3/32", or 1/16") between every adjacent piece.

1. How Strip-First Guillotine Packing Works in the Woodshop

Unlike laser cutters that can snake around L-shaped paths, a circular saw or cabinet table saw must make straight, through-and-through cuts from one edge of a board to the other:

  1. Step 1 — Sort by Largest Dimension: All requested rectangles are expanded by quantity and sorted from largest to smallest so major cabinet carcasses are placed before narrow nailers or toe-kicks.
  2. Step 2 — Shelf / Strip Allocation with Blade Kerf: Each part is placed into an existing horizontal rip strip if its width and remaining length (plus one blade kerf) fit; otherwise a new rip strip is opened on the current sheet or a fresh 4×8 sheet is started.
  3. Step 3 — Offcut & Yield Accounting: The calculator totals the net area of your finished parts against the gross area of all required sheets (32 sq ft per 4×8 panel) so you can price client bids accurately.

3. Frequently Asked Questions

Why must a plywood cut list calculator account for saw blade kerf?

Every pass of a table saw or track saw removes a slot of wood equal to the carbide tooth width—typically 1/8 inch (0.125" / 3.2 mm) for a full-kerf table saw blade or 3/32 inch (0.094" / 2.4 mm) for a thin-kerf circular saw blade. If you try to cut four 24-inch panels from a 96-inch sheet without accounting for three 1/8-inch kerfs, the fourth panel will come out 3/8 inch short at 23 5/8 inches.

What is the actual size of a 4x8 sheet of Baltic Birch or cabinet plywood?

Standard North American hardwood plywood, MDF, and OSB sheets measure 48 inches by 96 inches (1220 mm × 2440 mm). However, factory edges are often slightly out of square or bruised during shipping, so cabinetmakers typically trim 1/4 inch off both factory edges (leaving 47.5" × 95.5" usable stock).

How does 2D guillotine cutting differ from free nesting?

Table saws and panel saws only make straight edge-to-edge ('guillotine') rips and crosscuts across a sheet or sub-panel. Our optimizer groups parts into uniform-width rip strips first and then crosscuts parts along each strip, matching real cabinet-shop workflow.

Should plywood grain run along the length or width of cabinet parts?

On standard 4×8 veneer plywood, the wood grain runs parallel to the 96-inch (8-foot) long edge. Cabinet sides, tall doors, and visible bookshelf spans should always have their longest dimension aligned with the 96-inch sheet length for both visual grain continuity and maximum bending stiffness.

What is a good material yield percentage for sheet goods?

A sheet yield between 75% and 88% is considered highly efficient for custom cabinetry. Yields above 90% are rare once saw kerf, edge trimming, and grain orientation constraints are respected.
