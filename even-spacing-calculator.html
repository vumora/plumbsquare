Even Spacing & Baluster Calculator (Exact 1/16" On-Center Marks + IRC 4-Inch Rule)

Whether you are installing deck railing balusters between 4×4 posts, spacing cedar privacy fence slats, aligning dowel rods, or mounting kitchen island pendants, calculating even spacing by hand is notoriously error-prone. Why? Because when placing N spindles between two fixed posts with an equal gap against both posts, you are dividing into N + 1 spaces—not N! This calculator computes exact 1/16 inch (and millimeter) clear gaps, on-center steps, and cumulative tape-measure hook marks so every spindle lands dead-on.

1. Inside Posts (N+1 Gaps) vs. Flush Ends (N-1 Gaps)

There are two distinct ways builders distribute items across a fixed span:

  • Mode A — Equal Gap at Both Ends (Railings & Balusters): You already have a left post and a right post installed, and you want the air gap between the post and the first spindle to match the air gap between every spindle in the run. Here, Number of Gaps = N + 1.
  • Mode B — Items Flush at Both Ends (Slat Benches, Fence Posts, Studs): The first item sits flush at 0" and the final item sits flush at the end of the run. Here, Number of Gaps = N - 1.
[Mode A: Inside Posts] Clear Gap = (Span - (N × Width)) ÷ (N + 1)
[Mode B: Flush at Ends] Clear Gap = (Span - (N × Width)) ÷ (N - 1)
On-Center Pitch (OC) = Clear Gap + Item Width
Minimum Balusters for Max Gap (G_max) = ceil( (Span - G_max) ÷ (Width + G_max) )

2. Worked Example: 68-Inch Inside Deck Railing with 2×2 Wood Balusters

Suppose you set two 4×4 deck posts with an inside clear distance of 68 inches and you are installing standard 2×2 wood balusters (actual width 1 1/2") under the IRC 4-inch sphere rule (targeting a safe ≤ 3 3/4" clear gap):

  1. Minimum Baluster Count: Using ceil((68 - 3.75) ÷ (1.5 + 3.75)) = ceil(64.25 ÷ 5.25) = ceil(12.23) = 13 balusters.
  2. Total Wood Width: 13 × 1.5" = 19.5 inches.
  3. Remaining Air Space: 68" - 19.5" = 48.5 inches distributed across 13 + 1 = 14 gaps.
  4. Exact Clear Gap: 48.5" ÷ 14 = 3.464", which rounds to 3 7/16 inches (88 mm)—passing the 4-inch sphere inspection with half an inch to spare!

4. Frequently Asked Questions

How do you calculate even spacing between spindles, balusters, or slats?

To space N items evenly between two posts with equal gaps at both ends, there are N + 1 gaps. Subtract the combined width of all items (N × Item Width) from the inside opening length, then divide by (N + 1): Clear Gap = (Opening Length - (N × Item Width)) ÷ (N + 1). The on-center spacing from one spindle to the next is Clear Gap + Item Width.

What is the 4-inch sphere rule for deck balusters and stair railings?

Under IRC Section R312.1.3, openings in residential guards and railings on decks, porches, and stairs must not allow the passage of a 4-inch (102 mm) diameter sphere. Because wood balusters can flex slightly or shrink as they dry, professional builders set maximum baluster clear spacing at 3 1/2 to 3 3/4 inches.

How do I calculate the minimum number of balusters needed to pass building code?

Add one baluster width to your maximum allowed gap (for example, 1.5" spindle + 3.75" max gap = 5.25" on-center step). Divide your total inside railing length plus one spindle width by that on-center step and round UP to the next whole number of balusters.

What is the difference between on-center spacing and clear gap spacing?

Clear gap is the empty air space between the right edge of one picket and the left edge of the next picket. On-center (OC) spacing is the distance from the center of one picket to the center of the next picket (or from left edge to left edge), which always equals Clear Gap + 1 Picket Width.

Can I use this even spacing calculator for coat hooks, recessed lights, or shelf brackets?

Yes. Simply set the 'End Placement Mode' to 'Gap at Both Ends (Inside Posts)' and enter your fixture width (or 0.1 inch for point markers like recessed ceiling cans or cabinet knobs) to get exact 1/16 inch center marks across any span.
