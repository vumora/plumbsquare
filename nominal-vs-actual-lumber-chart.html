Nominal vs. Actual Lumber Sizes Chart (1×, 2×, 4×, 5/4 Decking & Hardwood Quarters)

Walk into any lumberyard or home center in the United States, Canada, the UK, or Australia and ask for a 2×4, and the board you carry out will measure 1 1/2 inches by 3 1/2 inches (38 mm × 89 mm). Why? Because the "nominal" name refers to the rough green log slice at the sawmill before kiln drying and four-side rotary planing (S4S — Surfaced Four Sides). Designing furniture, framing walls, or spacing balusters with nominal numbers instead of actual caliper dimensions will throw your entire layout off by inches.

1. Dimensional Framing Lumber (2× Softwood — SPF, Douglas Fir, Southern Pine)

All North American 2× framing lumber carries an actual surfaced thickness of 1 1/2 inches (38 mm). For widths up to 2×6, subtract 1/2 inch from the nominal width; for 2×8 through 2×12, subtract 3/4 inch:

2. 1× Finish Appearance Boards (Pine, Poplar, Oak, Cedar Trim)

All 1× finish appearance boards used in Board & Batten wainscoting and cabinet face frames measure 3/4 inch (19 mm) thick:

3. Posts, Timbers (4×4, 6×6) & 5/4 Radius-Edge Decking

  • 4×4 Post: Actual 3 1/2" × 3 1/2" (89 × 89 mm) — used for wood fence posts and deck guard posts.
  • 6×6 Post: Actual 5 1/2" × 5 1/2" (140 × 140 mm) — required under IRC R507 for elevated deck support posts and heavy gate posts.
  • 5/4×6 Decking: Actual 1" × 5 1/2" (25.4 × 140 mm) — standard radius-edge pressure-treated pine or cedar deck board.
